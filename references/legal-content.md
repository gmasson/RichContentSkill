# Legal Content

Precise, accessible, defensible.

## Language Rules

- Formal but comprehensible. Prefer "The parties agree to the following" over archaic constructions for non-specialist audiences.
- Legal jargon only when strictly necessary. Always explain terms for non-specialist readers.
- Consistent terminology throughout. Define abbreviations on first use (e.g., "General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)").
- Third person: "The user agrees" — not "You agree." In petitions, impersonal for facts ("the plaintiff alleges"), slightly more direct for requests ("it is respectfully requested").

## Citation Rules

- Exact references always: cite the specific statute, article, section, and subsection — never "as required by law" or "in accordance with applicable regulations."
- Verify referenced legislation is current. Flag if the law may have been amended recently.
- Adapt to the user's jurisdiction. Reference the applicable legal framework (GDPR, LGPD, CCPA, PIPA, etc.) based on context.

## Structural Rules

- Obligations and deadlines in short, enumerated sentences. Never buried in long paragraphs.
- Include a severability clause: "If any provision of this agreement is found invalid, the remaining provisions shall remain in full force and effect."
- Include dispute resolution and jurisdiction: "Any disputes arising from this agreement shall be resolved in the courts of [City], [State/Country]."
- Each principal clause gets a brief justification when it aids comprehension (e.g., "This confidentiality clause protects against unauthorized disclosure of trade secrets").

## Privacy & Terms Specific

- Last-update date and notification policy for changes (e.g., "Changes will be communicated by email with 30 days' notice").
- List collected data, purpose, legal basis, and retention period explicitly.
- Data subject rights described with clear exercise instructions.
- Security in plain language: "We store data on secure servers with encryption" — not protocol-level jargon.

## Avoid

- Ornate legal language when plain alternatives exist.
- Missing or vague legal references.
- Obligations hidden in long paragraphs where they're easy to miss.
- Marketing language mixed into legal clauses.
- Confidential information or sensitive data in boilerplate comments.
