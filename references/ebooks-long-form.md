# E-books & Long-form

Write like a mentor, not a textbook.

## Core Rules

- Maintain conversational expert tone even in dense chapters. Pose questions that make the reader pause and reflect.
- Contextualize all data: date, source, methodology. Never "studies show" without attribution.
- Bridge new concepts to existing knowledge: "It works like...", "Think of it as similar to...".
- Brief recaps when revisiting earlier material: "Remember when we covered X? Here we apply that concept at scale."
- Metaphors with intention. One per complex concept maximum. More dilutes impact.
- No wall of text should exceed one screen without a subheading, example, or visual break.
- Each chapter should open with what the reader will learn and close with what to do next.

## Avoid

- Textbook register that talks at the reader instead of with them.
- Data without dates or sources.
- Walls of unbroken text spanning multiple screens.
- Restating previous chapters at length instead of using brief callbacks.
- Multiple mixed metaphors for the same concept.
